Being broke costs more than being rich. And the real price isn't even money.
Think cheap saves you money? Watch.
Two people need boots. One pays two hundred dollars. They last ten years. The other buys fifty-dollar boots. They fall apart every winter. Ten years later? Five hundred dollars. And still wet feet.
That's the broke tax.
And you pay it everywhere. Late bill? Fee. Empty account? Overdraft fee. No savings? You borrow, and pay interest on your own groceries.
Meanwhile, someone with savings earns interest on the same money.
But here's the part nobody talks about.
The biggest broke tax isn't in your bank account. It's in your head.
Researchers found that money stress can hurt your thinking as much as losing a full night of sleep.
So when you're broke, every decision is made exhausted. And exhausted decisions cost money.
That's the trap. Less money. Worse choices. More fees. Less money.
The way out isn't a bigger paycheck. It's a small cushion. A few hundred dollars set aside buys back your sleep, your focus, your choices.
Because the most expensive thing about being broke is what it does to your mind.
